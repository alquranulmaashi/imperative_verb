افْعَلْ

The Imperative Verb

فِعْلُ الأَمْر — المُجَرَّدُ وَالمَزِيدُ، ثُلَاثِيًّا وَرُبَاعِيًّا

فِعْلُ الأَمْر
Write! · Go! · Seek forgiveness!

The imperative is built from the present tense of “you”. Drop the prefix, trim the ending, and — only sometimes — add a hamza in front. Learn the one rule and you can command any verb: bare or augmented, three letters or four, with verses from the Qur’an.

اُكْتُبْ اِذْهَبْ أَخْرِجْ عَلِّمْ
Build it

مِنَ المُضَارِعِ إِلَى الأَمْرFour steps from the present tense

1
Take the present tense of “you”: تَكْتُبُ.
2
Drop the prefix تَـ → كْتُبُ.
3
Make it jussive: the final ḍamma becomes sukūn (كْتُبْ); the ending ـَانِ → ـَا, ـُونَ → ـُوا, ـِينَ → ـِي lose their ن. Only the feminine plural keeps it: ـْنَ.
4
Does the word now start with a sukūn? Then put a hamza in front. If it starts with a vowel already, add nothing.

The imperative exists only for you. To command “he”, “they” or “us”, use لِـ + the jussive present: وَلْيَكْتُب بَّيْنَكُمْ كَاتِبٌ بِالْعَدْلِ — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:282, “and let a scribe write between you in justice.”

i
A waṣl hamza is dropped when something comes before it, so you only say it at the start: اُكْتُبْ but وَاكْتُبْ (wa-ktub). The qaṭ‘ hamza of Form IV never drops: وَأَخْرِجْ.
From the Qur’an — all three vowels in one verse
يَا مَرْيَمُ اقْنُتِي لِرَبِّكِ وَاسْجُدِي وَارْكَعِي مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ
Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:43 — “O Mary, be devout to your Lord, prostrate and bow with those who bow.” At the start you would say اُقْنُتِي and اُسْجُدِي (ḍamma — the present is يَقْنُتُ، يَسْجُدُ) but اِرْكَعِي (kasra — the present is يَرْكَعُ, with fatḥa). The Qur’anic spelling does not mark the hamza’s vowel.
Bare three-letter verbs

الثُّلَاثِيُّ المُجَرَّداُفْعُلْ · اِفْعِلْ · اِفْعَلْ

The ruleThe hamza’s vowel follows the middle vowel of the present: ḍamma → اُ; kasra or fatḥa → اِ. So you must know each verb’s present — the three faces from the previous lesson.
اُ hamzaـُوا ending
From the Qur’an
وَاكْتُبْ لَنَا فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً
ḍamma, you (m.) — Sūrah al-A‘rāf, 7:156: “and decree for us good in this world.”
اذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · اذْهَبَا إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · يَا بَنِيَّ اذْهَبُوا فَتَحَسَّسُوا
fatḥa verb, you (m.), you two, you (pl.) — Sūrah Ṭā-Hā, 20:24 and 20:43; Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:87. The same verb in three forms.
فَكُلِي وَاشْرَبِي وَقَرِّي عَيْنًا
fatḥa verb, you (f.) — Sūrah Maryam, 19:26: “so eat and drink and be comforted.”
وَاذْكُرْنَ مَا يُتْلَىٰ فِي بُيُوتِكُنَّ مِنْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ
ḍamma, you (f. pl.) — Sūrah al-Aḥzāb, 33:34: “and remember what is recited in your homes of Allah’s verses.”
قَالَ اهْبِطَا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا
kasra verb, you two — Sūrah Ṭā-Hā, 20:123: “He said: ‘Go down from it, both of you, together.’”
رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَا
kasra, you (m.) — Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:147: “Our Lord, forgive us our sins.”
اضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ
kasra, you (m.) — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:60: “Strike the stone with your staff.”
ارْجِعُوا وَرَاءَكُمْ فَالْتَمِسُوا نُورًا
kasra, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Ḥadīd, 57:13: “Go back behind you and seek light.”
ادْخُلُوهَا بِسَلَامٍ آمِنِينَ
ḍamma, you (pl.) + هَا — Sūrah al-Ḥijr, 15:46: “Enter it in peace, secure.” An attached object pronoun lands on the end of the imperative, just as it does on the past tense.
رَبَّنَا افْتَحْ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَ قَوْمِنَا بِالْحَقِّ
fatḥa, you (m.) — Sūrah al-A‘rāf, 7:89: “Our Lord, decide between us and our people in truth.”
Bare four-letter verbs

الرُّبَاعِيُّ المُجَرَّدفَعْلِلْ

The ruleThe present of فَعْلَلَ is تُفَعْلِلُ. Drop the تُـ and the word already starts with a vowel (فَـ) — so no hamza: دَحْرِجْ.
ـُوا ending
i
I haven’t found a Qur’anic imperative of a bare four-letter verb that I can confirm, so none is shown. (The Qur’an does have the present يُوَسْوِسُ and the passive زُلْزِلُوا.)
Forms II · III · IV

Augmented by one letter: فَعِّلْ · فَاعِلْ · أَفْعِلْ

The ruleII and III start with a vowel once the prefix goes, so there is no hamza. Form IV has lost its first letter to the prefix, so it takes back a true hamza qaṭ‘ with fatḥa.
أَ hamzaـُوا ending
From the Qur’an
وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ · وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ
Form II, you (m.) — Sūrah al-Muddaththir, 74:3–4: “and your Lord, glorify; and your garments, purify.”
فَسَبِّحْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الْعَظِيمِ
Form II, you (m.) — Sūrah al-Wāqi‘ah, 56:74: “So glorify the name of your Lord, the Most Great.”
فَذَكِّرْ إِن نَّفَعَتِ الذِّكْرَىٰ
Form II, you (m.) — Sūrah al-A‘lā, 87:9: “So remind, if the reminder should benefit.”
وَقَدِّمُوا لِأَنفُسِكُمْ
Form II, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:223: “and send [good] ahead for yourselves.”
وَجَاهِدُوا بِأَمْوَالِكُمْ وَأَنفُسِكُمْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ
Form III, you (pl.) — Sūrah at-Tawbah, 9:41: “and strive with your wealth and your lives in the cause of Allah.”
وَقَاتِلُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ
Form III, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:190: “Fight in the cause of Allah those who fight you.”
وَسَارِعُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ
Form III, you (pl.) — Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:133: “and hasten to forgiveness from your Lord.”
أَخْرِجُوا آلَ لُوطٍ مِّن قَرْيَتِكُمْ
Form IV, you (pl.) — Sūrah an-Naml, 27:56: “Expel the family of Lot from your town.”
أَرْسِلْهُ مَعَنَا غَدًا يَرْتَعْ وَيَلْعَبْ
Form IV, you (m.) + هُ — Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:12: “Send him with us tomorrow, that he may enjoy himself and play.”
وَأَدْخِلْ يَدَكَ فِي جَيْبِكَ تَخْرُجْ بَيْضَاءَ
Form IV, you (m.) — Sūrah an-Naml, 27:12: “and put your hand into your garment; it will come out white.”
وَأَحْسِنُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُحْسِنِينَ
Form IV, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:195: “and do good; indeed Allah loves the doers of good.”
Forms V · VI

The تَـ group: تَفَعَّلْ · تَفَاعَلْ

The ruleThe present has two ت’s (تَتَعَلَّمُ). Drop one prefix and the other is left — the word starts with a vowel, so no hamza: تَعَلَّمْ.
ـُوا ending
From the Qur’an
إِذَا قِيلَ لَكُمْ تَفَسَّحُوا فِي الْمَجَالِسِ فَافْسَحُوا
Form V, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Mujādilah, 58:11: “When it is said to you, ‘Make room in the gatherings,’ then make room.”
وَتَصَدَّقْ عَلَيْنَا
Form V, you (m.) — Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:88: “and be charitable to us.”
فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللَّهِ
Form V, you (m.) — Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:159: “and when you have decided, rely upon Allah.” (Its root starts with a و, which does not change here.)
وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ
Form VI, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Mā’idah, 5:2: “and cooperate in righteousness and piety.” (Its middle root letter is a و; it behaves normally in Form VI.)
Forms VII · VIII · X

The hamzat-waṣl group: اِنْفَعِلْ · اِفْتَعِلْ · اِسْتَفْعِلْ

The ruleWithout the prefix each begins with a sukūn (نْفَعِلُ، فْتَعِلُ، سْتَفْعِلُ), so a hamzat waṣl with kasra comes back in front.
اِ hamzaـُوا ending
From the Qur’an
انطَلِقُوا إِلَىٰ مَا كُنتُم بِهِ تُكَذِّبُونَ
Form VII, you (pl.) — Sūrah al-Mursalāt, 77:29: “Go to that which you used to deny.”
وَاسْجُدْ وَاقْتَرِب
Form VIII, you (m.) — Sūrah al-‘Alaq, 96:19: “Prostrate and draw near.”
فَاسْتَمِعُوا لَهُ وَأَنصِتُوا
Form VIII (the tricky اسْتَمَعَ), you (pl.) — Sūrah al-A‘rāf, 7:204: “then listen to it and be silent.”
فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ
Form VIII, you (pl.) + هُ — Sūrah al-Mā’idah, 5:90: “so avoid it, that you may succeed.”
فَقُلْتُ اسْتَغْفِرُوا رَبَّكُمْ إِنَّهُ كَانَ غَفَّارًا
Form X, you (pl.) — Sūrah Nūḥ, 71:10: “I said: ‘Ask forgiveness of your Lord; indeed He is ever Forgiving.’”
وَاسْتَغْفِرِ اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا
Form X, you (m.) — Sūrah an-Nisā’, 4:106: “and ask Allah’s forgiveness; indeed Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.”
Doubled patterns

Form IX اِفْعَلَّ and 4-letter اِفْعَلِلَّ

The ruleA hamzat waṣl with kasra, then the doubled last letter: packed before a vowel ending (اِحْمَرُّوا), unpacked before ـْنَ (اِحْمَرِرْنَ).
اِ hamzaـُّوا ending
i
For “you (m.)” a doubled verb also allows an unpacked form: اِحْمَرِرْ beside اِحْمَرَّ (like مُدَّ / اُمْدُدْ); the table shows the packed one. Colours and defects are rarely commanded, and I have no Qur’anic imperative for these patterns.
Four-letter verbs, augmented

مَزِيدُ الرُّبَاعِيّتَفَعْلَلْ · اِفْعَنْلِلْ

The rules تَفَعْلَلَ keeps its own تَـ → no hamza: تَدَحْرَجْ.
افْعَنْلَلَ starts with a sukūn once the prefix goes → hamzat waṣl, kasra: اِحْرَنْجِمْ.
اِ hamzaـُوا ending
i
These are rare, literary verbs and I have no Qur’anic imperative for them; the pattern simply follows the one rule.
